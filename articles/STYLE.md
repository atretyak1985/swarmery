# Style

How the author writes about swarmery, and the facts every article can lean on.
Derived on 2026-10-06 from the seven posts already on Substack and from the
author's answers in the first `/write-article` interview. Update it when the
author edits a draft or comments on one.

## Voice

- First person singular: "I", never "we".
- Plain, spoken sentences. The author thinks and drafts in Ukrainian and the
  English is a translation, so it should read as a direct translation of
  speech: "And", "But" and "So" open sentences, and nothing is polished into
  idiom he would not use.
- Says where he was wrong or where something is unfinished, in so many words:
  "To be fair", "I have to say honestly", "for now this is a plan and not a
  finished thing".
- Explains a mechanism with one everyday comparison (a student at an exam, a
  renovation), then returns to the project.
- Asks the reader's question for them before answering it: "So how much does
  it cost?"

## Shape of a post

- 1,000 to 2,300 words.
- Opens on one line that is also the caption of the first image.
- The key sentence of a section comes back once as a line of its own. On
  Substack these are image captions and pull quotes. Keep them to a handful.
- Recent posts carry no section headings; images break the text.
- Says how the post follows from the previous one.
- Ends with what the next post will be about, a line that the code is open on
  GitHub, and "Previous post: <title>".

## Process

- The author's working language is Ukrainian. The interview is held in
  Ukrainian and the article is published in English.
- Every article exists in two versions: `article.uk.md`, which the author reads
  and edits, and `article.md` in English, which is published. An edit to one is
  carried into the other (asked for on 2026-10-06, after the first article
  arrived in English only).
- Still owed to readers: a post on lessons and forecasts with results,
